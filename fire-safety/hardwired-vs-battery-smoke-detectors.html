Hardwired vs. Battery Smoke Alarms

Reviewed and updated: September 19, 2026. This is general fire-safety information, not an installation plan or a substitute for the alarm instructions, local fire authority, or adopted code.

Power source and sensing technology are separate choices. A hardwired alarm receives household electrical power; a battery alarm uses a replaceable or sealed long-life battery. Either type still needs correct placement, a working test, and replacement at the manufacturer’s end-of-life date.

Hardwired alarms

Hardwired alarms commonly include a backup battery so the alarm can keep operating during an outage. They may also be interconnected. Do not assume every hardwired alarm has the same backup or communication behavior: check the exact product instructions and existing system. USFA advises that qualified electricians install hardwired alarms.

Battery-powered alarms

Battery alarms can provide practical coverage where wiring is not available. USFA notes that battery-powered alarms may use a disposable 9-volt battery or a nonreplaceable 10-year lithium battery. A sealed-battery alarm still needs monthly testing and full replacement according to its end-of-life instructions; it is not maintenance-free.

What should control the choice

  • Required locations: inside bedrooms, outside sleeping areas, and every level.
  • Local building, rental, and renovation requirements.
  • Whether the current system must remain interconnected.
  • Manufacturer compatibility and recognized testing-laboratory listing.
  • Whether a licensed or otherwise qualified electrician is needed for the work.

Do not remove a hardwired alarm, cap wires, or replace it with an unrelated battery model just because the new unit is easier to install. That can defeat interconnection or violate local requirements.

Maintenance is not optional

Test every alarm monthly with its test button. Follow the manufacturer’s battery instructions; USFA’s general guidance calls for at least annual replacement of disposable 9-volt and hardwired backup batteries. Replace the complete alarm at the manufacturer’s date, generally 10 years from manufacture.
